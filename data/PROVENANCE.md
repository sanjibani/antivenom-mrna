# Provenance of every sequence in this repository

No sequence enters this repository without a source. This file records where
each raw file came from, what it is, and its SHA-256 checksum, so that any
reader can re-download it and confirm they got the same bytes.

Raw downloads live in `data/reference/raw/`. Derived files live in
`data/reference/` and are regenerated by `scripts/prepare_reference_data.py`.

## The candidate table

`data/candidates.tsv` is an output of `scripts/candidate_tracker.py`, generated
from the curated rows in `src/mrna/candidates.py`. It contains no sequences. It
records, for each published antibody candidate, where the variable-region
sequence can be found, which for most of them is nowhere that can be opened
from a public database.

The table was compiled by a survey of the published literature on 2026-11-01,
using the RCSB full-text search API, PubMed, Google Patents and the IMPACT
Global Health catalogue *Investigational candidates for snakebite
envenoming*. Every row carries a citation key, and the citations are printed
in full at the bottom of `docs/m4_candidate_tracker.md`.

| Source | What was taken from it |
| --- | --- |
| RCSB Protein Data Bank | The structural candidates: 8DA1, 8D9Z, 8DA0, 8D9Y (Centi-LNX-D09), 8V13 (Centi-SNX-B03), 8SXP (95Mat5), 9GCN (TPL1158_01_C09), 9HUO, 9FYT, 9HXO (A01), 9CLP (H11) |
| PubMed | The evidence statements: Kumar et al. 2024 (PMID 38154407), Samanta et al. 2026 (PMID 42715346), Li et al. 2019 (PMID 31689425), Ledsgaard et al. 2023, Glanville et al. 2025 |
| Google Patents | The patent status column: US20240294618A1 and EP4319816A4, assigned to Centivax Inc |
| IMPACT Global Health, *Investigational candidates for snakebite envenoming* | The two polyclonal candidates, PCR ID 1708 and PCR ID 1864 |

The table is a survey of the public record, not an exhaustive census. A row
saying that no sequence was located means that none was located during the
survey, not that none exists.

## The payload table

`src/mrna/payload.py` names six antibody payloads and says where each one
comes from. Two of them, E113 and TPL1158_01_C09, are single chains and are
transcribed as they stand. The other four, Centi-LNX-D09, Centi-SNX-B03, H11
and A01, are Fabs or two-chain antibodies, and their heavy and light chains
are kept as separate records rather than concatenated: joining them without a
linker would produce a molecule that no publication describes.

Only the antibody chains are written out. The toxins in the same deposited
complexes are the targets, and they are already carried by the candidate
table in `src/mrna/candidates.py` with their own citations.

| Payload | Format | Chains | Source entry | Designed in M5 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| E113 | scFv | 1 of 239 aa | 8IA6 entity 1 | yes, and reproduced byte for byte from M3 |
| TPL1158_01_C09 | VHH | 1 of 123 aa | 9GCN entity 1 | yes |
| Centi-LNX-D09 | Fab | 2, light 214 aa and heavy 237 aa | 8DA1 entities 1 and 2 | no, two chains |
| Centi-SNX-B03 | Fab | 2, heavy 230 aa and light 215 aa | 8V13 entities 1 and 2 | no, two chains |
| H11 | Fab | 2, heavy 217 aa and light 217 aa | 9CLP entities 3 and 4 | no, two chains |
| A01 | mAb-IgG | 2, light 144 aa and heavy 117 aa | 9HUO entities 2 and 3 | no, two chains |

The chain lengths are what the Protein Data Bank holds, and the test suite
asserts every one of them.

## Checksums

Regenerate with:

```bash
uv run python scripts/prepare_reference_data.py
```

The script prints the SHA-256 of every raw file at the end of each run.
